# Hellfeather — Story Bible

*The Year of the Moult.* A dark-fantasy ARPG set in the world of **DSC**, roughly six centuries before Jay Carlson's case. Same magic, same Inquisition, same crystal, same two "children". This is the story of how the thing beneath Round and Sweet was first put in a box, and of the woman whose consent closed the lid.

---

## 1. Premise

For nine days black feathers have fallen on the Fenmarch. The ducks of the fen have turned vicious, the ravens of Ravenford gorge themselves on the feathers and grow sick, and in the hospice on the hill a disgraced weaver named **Jessa Brune** waits to die after confessing to the slaughter of twenty-seven people at Hobb's Mill.

**Wren Carrow**, thief-taker of Ravenford, coughing grey from an old mine sickness and living on borrowed months, is hired by a polite stranger named **Matthew the Journeyman** for fifty silver. He does not want to know *whether* Jessa killed them. He wants to know **why**.

The answer runs through every parish in the realm: an **Essence Tithe** that drains the poor, the sick and the unwanted to feed a failing seal over something that lives outside intention. The founders of the Tithe skim the flow through grey rings and have not aged in forty years. And everyone who has ever offered Wren a solution has needed her hand to do the thing they cannot.

**Themes, carried over from DSC:**
- *"Necessary"* is the word you hide bodies inside.
- Results do not wash the hands that produced them.
- Consent is the only lock that holds. The constructs cannot force, only open doors and wait for you to walk through.

**Tone:** grim, wry, human. Wren has Jay's dry mouth and Jay's flaw: she needs every story to end with herself as the one who solved it.

**Laws of Weaving.** The Cloister of Looms teaches Orra's two laws: *a weaver shall not harm another weaver or a living person,* and *no inscription without consent.* Every weaver in the realm breaks them for a living.

**Why Ravenford is safe.** Ravenford's ravens eat the black feathers. The town's Ravenkeeper feeds them what townsfolk bring in, so the moult never settles inside the walls. This is the hub's in-world reason to exist, and it gives the player a recurring turn-in (feathers for raven favour).

## 3. Framing device: the Confession

Each act ends with an **Interlude**: a short exchange between Wren and **Inquisitor Rahel Dar** in a stone room lit by one candle. Wren's voice is calm and her answers are dry. Rahel never shows her the room.

The final interlude reveals that Rahel is speaking to **the Egg**. Wren's will is the clasp that holds it shut, and she gave this confession from inside it. Rahel orders the Egg sealed beneath the bakehouse and the confession kept by her family. Six hundred years later, a Dar sits across a table from Jay Carlson and asks almost the same questions.

### ACT I — The Moult
*Ravenford and the Fenmarch. Wet lowlands, reed rivers, one road to the world.*

**Story.** Matthew hires Wren in the Crooked Raven. At Saint Orra's Hospice, Jessa grabs Wren's arm. The pain enters without breaking the skin, and Wren sees silver threads running out of every bed toward the mountains. *"Find the Grey Sister. Sever the bearers first. Then the heart. Don't break the box."* Jessa dies. The Tithe-mark lives in Wren now. In Jessa's garret Wren finds the reasons and the warnings, and on the stair she meets a grey-coated woman whose shadow leans the wrong way. Beneath Hobb's Mill the truth is plain: bearers chained to iron frames, essence pouring through a grey ring on Goodwife Mardra's hand and spilling into the millpond, where the fen ducks drink it and turn. Wren kills Mardra and performs her first Severing. Mardra's daughter watches from the mill stair. It is **Lise Hobb**, the woman Wren sent through a window two years ago.

### ACT II — Rooms Remember
*The Hospice Road, the weaving town of Thornwick, and the south wing of Saint Orra's.*

**Story.** The threads lead to Thornwick and the **Cloister of Looms**, the school that broke Jessa. Wren learns weaving by ruining things, and learns the Laws by watching everyone break them. The Thornwick tithe-house ledgers list people as codes. Of the twenty-six names Jessa crossed out at the mill, seven were her victims; the Red Cuffs added the rest to her count. Matthew's rented rooms hold a file on Wren: *"Persistent heroic self-conception despite contradictory conduct."* The girl Wren "rescued" two years ago had agreed to the whole thing. There was no kidnapper, only a test, with Lise beneath the window. At midnight in the hospice records cellar Vale shows her **DECEASED — TRANSFERRED**, and Jessa's admission, signed by Matthew six hours before her arrest. Beside *COMPATIBLE SUCCESSOR* is Wren's name. In the locked south wing **Corvin Hale the Ringwright** is still alive, kept to repair the rings he regrets making. Wren frees him. He tells her about the Box at Mount Corvane and who opened it. Vale does not deny it: *"I opened the box."*

*The valley of the River Gall, the port of Gallmouth, and the scars Jessa left.*

**Story.** Corvin maps the network: **eleven tithe-houses** feed the Box. Jessa broke four by force and left scars that grow silver roots and give whole streets the same dreams. ### ACT IV — The Box
*Mount Corvane: the Orran Abbey, the Grey Cough mines, the Rookery Crags.*

**Story.** Every thread converges on the mountain where **Saint Orra** sealed the thin place three hundred years ago with her own life and her own consent. Wren learns where her cough comes from. The **Grey Cough** is the Box's breath. Miners who breathe the dust become sick, and they become *compatible*. Wren hauled ore here as a girl. Forty years ago, as Sister Vale of the Orran Abbey, Vale led **Abbess Ysolde Venn** to open the reliquary and cure the plague. The miners rose healthy. Then the seal began to starve, and Ysolde learned it could be fed by the sick and skimmed by the faithful. That was the first Tithe and the first grey ring. Vale regretted it. Matthew did not. In the crypt, **the Orran Voice** (Orra's last intention, inscribed into stone) tells Wren what Vale never would: the box was never meant to be fed. It was meant to be **kept**, by a will that consents to hold it. In Orra's tomb lies the **Soulcast Quarrel**, the one object made before the box closed. It is the only thing that can break the Box. Matthew wants Wren to carry it. Vale cannot touch it. Then the reliquary chamber turns out to be empty. **Ser Cerelle** and the Red Cuffs carried the Box down to Bellhaven on the Abbess's order, and they left Cerelle behind to finish things. Corvin Hale dies on her glass dagger.

### ACT V — The Ringed Matriarch
*Bellhaven, seat of the Holy Inquisition and the city that will one day be Jay Carlson's.*

**Story.** The Box is under the **Round and Sweet Bakehouse**, whose master, **Brother Tobiah**, is a founder of the Tithe. Tobiah offers Wren a cure: transfer the Tithe-mark into a dying bearer and receive a ring of her own. He gives her the key to the flour door anyway, *because cowardice and rebellion sometimes share a coat.* Wren's body is failing, and black honey would hold her up. Rahel Dar draws the Red Cuffs to the front of the Chapterhouse while Wren and Vale slip under the icing. They free a braid of fifty-eight bearers. ### ACT VI — The Hellfeather
*Through the Reliquary door, into the place outside intention.*

**Story.** Wren follows them down. The world past the door has no fixed shape. Lattice Step loses minutes, then hours. Matthew opens doors, and each one is a room from Wren's past: the inn window, the millpond, the alley with Emeric. He cannot touch her. He only keeps showing her what she did and asking who did it. Vale is coming apart, held to the world by a few threads. She gives Wren the last thing she knows: *"He cannot harm you. He needs permission. Whatever he offers, refuse."* At the bottom, in a cathedral made of eggshell, **the Unfledged** wears Lise Hobb like a coat. It has her voice and her new legs, and it has her reason for hating Wren.
Wren holds the Soulcast Quarrel, which can break the Box forever. Matthew has spent forty years making sure she would. But the Orran Voice told her the Box is **kept**, not fed. Above, Rahel Dar holds the vault door with Orra's Chain: *hold what consents to being held.*

## 7. Enemies — difficulty curve and family index

### Difficulty curve
Enemies get more complicated act by act. A returning enemy keeps its old moves and gains the next act's tools; its entry says what it adds.

| Act | Moves per enemy | Effects on the player | New ideas introduced |

| **I** | **1** (elites 2) | Damage and small knockback only. No roots, blinds, theft, DoT or control loss. | Chase and hit, a clear wind-up, simple ranged, enemies that are passive until hit, freeing bearers instead of killing them. |

| **VI** | 3+ | Rule-breaking: time, shadow and environment. | Enemies that repeat your past, attack from your shadow, mimic, or can only be beaten by a non-combat action. |

## 9. Systems this story implies

- **Necessary tally:** a hidden counter, raised by draining people, drinking black honey, leaving bearers in the gas, accepting Tobiah's cure, or equipping Ysolde's hand. It decides which endings Act VI offers.
- **Severing interactions:** release, return, close at relay sockets. A short hold-to-channel interaction while enemies press in. The closing stroke must face the bearer, and doing it wrong causes an *Inversion* event.
- **Runes from Act I, crafting from Act II:** runes are in play from the first act. They drop from enemies, are sold by Moth at the Lantern & Moth, and are given as quest rewards (I-6 gives the Dash rune). Act II-2 unlocks **inscription crafting**: carving runes from Blank Runes and inscribing items. That uses the existing `Runes.dk` / `RuneScreen.dk` board. Lore: the rune board *is* the Tithe-mark on Wren's skin. Each rune is a mark set into it, and each level opens a new cell because the mark spreads. The War and Wind themes read as Orran marks of *Strike* and *Step*. See "Runes in Act I" below.
- **Action bar mapping:** *Dash* is **Lattice Step**. *War Cry* fits as **Thief-Taker's Shout**. *Cleave* stays Cleave. *Tithe Touch* and *Orra's Chain* are new late-game actions.
- **Feather economy:** black feathers drop everywhere and turn in at Edda in Ravenford, the hub's in-world reason to keep coming home.

## 10. Canon links back to DSC

- The Egg sits beneath Round and Sweet for six centuries, until Alex Matt rebuilds its failing shell as the crystal and Risa Venn learns to tithe it again.
- The Soulcast Quarrel's head becomes Alex's soul-cast bullet. Its intention was made before the Box closed, which is why it is the one thing that can break the crystal.
- Vale's form is anchored by the Egg's remains, which is why Luna's survival depends on the crystal in DSC.
- The Orran Eye becomes Alex's maker mark. The Barred Eye returns on Risa's ring.
- *Hold what consents to being held* is Orra's Chain. Central Oversight's chains in DSC are its descendants, and the Dar family has kept Wren's confession since the Year of the Moult.
- Jesse Brown repeats Jessa Brune's life almost exactly, because the network repeats itself. *Each expansion was temporary. Each became essential.*
